## In two minutes

There is a study worth knowing about before you spend money on anything else.

Researchers ran 4,000 hotel queries through Google's AI Mode across eight cities and tracked where
the resulting clicks actually went. The result upends what most hoteliers assume:

- **79% of hotel links stayed inside Google, on Google Business Profiles.**
- **Travel booking sites supplied 46.6% of the citations but received only 3.6% of the clicks.**

Read those two lines together and a clean division of labour appears. Your listings on MakeMyTrip,
Booking.com and Tripadvisor are what make you *mentionable* — they are the reference material the
machine reads. But your **Google Business Profile is where the guest actually goes**.

One of those costs you 18 to 25% commission on every booking. The other is free, you already own
it, and most hotels have never finished filling it in.

There is a second reason this is the highest-return work available. Google's conversational Maps
feature launched in India in March 2026 and reads Business Profiles directly. Apple's rebuilt Siri
now runs on a Google model, so an iPhone asking for a hotel is reading the same data. And Android
phones in India began defaulting their assistant to Gemini in September 2026.

One free asset. Four different places guests are asking.

## The detail

### What the study found

The [AI Mode hotel click study](https://hotelrank.ai/research/google-ai-mode-hotel-study-2026)
examined how Google's conversational search surfaces hotels and where users go next. The headline
split — 79% of clicks to Google Business Profiles, 3.6% to online travel agencies despite those
agencies supplying nearly half the underlying citations — is the most actionable finding published
about hotel AI visibility so far.

It is worth being careful here: this is a third-party study and we have not independently verified
its methodology. But it is directionally consistent with something anyone can observe by asking
Gemini for a hotel and watching what it links to.

### Why Google keeps the guest close

Google has a structural interest that happens to align with yours. When someone asks an assistant
for a hotel, Google would rather the answer resolve inside its own surfaces — Maps, Business
Profiles, its hotel module — than send the user to a competing travel site. For an independent
hotel this is unusually good news, because a Business Profile is free, directly controlled by the
owner, and does not charge commission.

Compare this with what happened on 27 August 2026, when Google began completing hotel bookings
inside AI Mode. That launched with ten partners — five booking platforms and five hotel chains
including Hilton, Wyndham and Choice — in the United States only, in English only.
[Independent hotels were not on the list](https://skift.com/2026/08/27/googles-agentic-hotel-booking-tool-comes-to-ai-mode/).
The paid, transactional channel is currently closed to you. The free, discovery channel is wide
open.

### What "complete" actually means

Most hotels have claimed a Business Profile and stopped. Completing it means:

- **Every field populated** — category, full address, geo-coordinates, phone, website, check-in and
  check-out times, amenities, accessibility, payment methods.
- **Real photographs, in volume, refreshed.** Rooms as they actually look, the property in
  different seasons, food, common areas. Photographs uploaded by guests count too.
- **Review volume and recency.** Both matter to what assistants surface. A property with 40 recent
  reviews outranks one with 200 from four years ago.
- **Replies to reviews, including the difficult ones.** Hotels that respond are measurably more
  likely to receive enquiries, and the replies themselves become text the machine reads.
- **Consistency with everywhere else.** The same name, the same address format, the same room
  names, the same policies as on your website and your OTA listings. Contradictions cause a
  property to be quietly omitted rather than demoted, because the system cannot state anything
  about you with confidence.
- **Products, services and posts** kept current — these are additional structured fields most
  hotels never touch.

### The free channel most hotels never switch on

While you are in Google's ecosystem, there is a second free asset: **free booking links**. These
let your own direct rate appear next to the OTA rates in Google's hotel module, at zero cost per
click and zero commission.

They require three things: a claimed Business Profile, a matched hotel listing, and a **connected
rate feed** — which means your booking engine or channel manager must push live rates to Google.
That last requirement is why most independents do not have it, and it is a question worth putting
to your current vendor directly.

Volume through free booking links typically runs at 15 to 25% of what paid hotel ads deliver.
Small, but free and compounding.

### The one thing that makes this urgent rather than merely sensible

Nothing described here is difficult or expensive. That is precisely the problem with leaving it.

The signals that decide who gets recommended — review volume, consistency, structured data,
citations — **accumulate**. A property that becomes legible this season starts building a record
that the one waiting until next season cannot instantly match. The advantage is not the work
itself, which anyone can copy in a week. It is the months of accumulated signal that the early
property has and the late one does not.
